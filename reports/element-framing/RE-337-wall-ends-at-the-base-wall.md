Each is drawn from its record box with the joins' trim (RE-26): `JoinTrimStart` 0.7136 ft (0.2175 m), `JoinTrimEnd` 0. Each has an edited elevation profile, so its native record holds five or six `GLine`s and `native_walls` reads no curve for it, and the passes that read the native curve (RE-222, RE-252, RE-322) leave it out (65 of the office's walls as built have more than one `GLine`).

The wall's line ends at y 75.0149 ft, the centreline of the Level_01 wall 1344124 (1.4272 ft thick, 183.07 to 195.87 ft), which is the record box's end before the trim. The trim solver takes the walls its record names that are perpendicular, whose centreline lies on the end, that span the wall's centreline and that overlap it in elevation by any amount: 1344124 overlaps the wall (174.21 to 183.73 ft) by 0.66 ft, and the end is cut half its thickness in, to its face at 75.7285 ft. But Revit's wall at its base is cut against the walls its end step names, `JoinEndGStep.m_geomJoined` of end 0: 1493966, a Basement wall, 169.62 to 182.09 ft, 1.5584 ft thick about y 74.6391, whose far face is y 75.4183 ft, and the line's end (75.0149) lies inside that wall's thickness, a T joint with it (and 3490093 and the abutting 1347199). Revit's body steps up the end: the scorer's horizontal faces of 2057668 are a step of 0.1601 ft2 at 182.087 ft (the top of 1493966) between y 75.4341 and 75.6293, and a step of 0.0943 ft2 at 183.071 ft (the top of 3490093, the base of 1344124) between 75.6293 and 75.7443 (75.6135 is the face of 3490093, 75.7285 that of 1344124).

## 2. What was tried and dropped

- The end's walls from the native record (`m_geomJoined`, in the T-joint pass of RE-222): the four walls have no native curve (above), and on Autodesk's Snowdon Towers Architectural, the one model it moved, there is nothing to score it against.
- The record solver's candidates (RE-26): taking the face of the one named wall that holds the base where every wall on the end lies above the base moved the four walls as built and none at permit. It also moved 25 of Autodesk's sample projects (every `rac_basic` save from 2016 to 2027, the `rme` samples and Snowdon Towers Architectural), one held-out model (`bimspeed-gdynia-2021`) and the office's wall 1347199, none of them with a Revit export to score it against. The condition holds on many more ends than the four, so it was dropped.
- A rule on the edited profile alone (the end drawn to the face of the one perpendicular wall beneath that holds the base, within its thickness), run at `f3ebce31` (Measure 41941971619), did not reach the as-built walls: their record boxes turn by the angle the record stores in single precision, 1.5e-7 radian off a half turn (3.1415925 for pi), and the rule required a quarter turn to within 1e-9. The as-built office was byte for byte what `main` writes. At permit, where the stored angles are exact quarter turns, it moved walls 2057668 and 2057670.
- The same rule with a tolerance of 1e-6 of a quarter turn and no condition on the join (Measure 41944433297, `33153cff`) moved two walls on the office as built and the office at permit as intended, and one model of the 88 outside the office: Snowdon Towers Architectural, whose soffit wall 2033183 ("Soffit - Beam Wrap (Both Sides)", an edited profile) had both its ends drawn to the faces of walls 748858 and 748873, which hold its base, 0.0508 m (2 in) further out each. Revit's export of that file (local only: the IFC4 export in `_corpus_candidates/snowdon/`) gives the wall's axis and body 6.86435 m long, which the base wrote exactly; the rule made it 6.96595 m. Its `JoinTrimStart` and `JoinTrimEnd` are 0: the wall stops short of the others and no join cut it. The Basement walls are cut 0.7136 ft by the join and their box ends inside the wall beneath, which is the condition kept (section 3).

## 3. The rule

What the four walls share that the walls beside them do not is the edited elevation profile (a `WallProfileOpening` voids each) and a record box whose end lies inside the wall beneath, cut back by a join trim. For a wall with an edited profile that is a rectangle along a model axis (to 1e-6 of a quarter turn) and has `JoinTrimStart` and `JoinTrimEnd`, an end is drawn to the face of the other rectangles along the perpendicular axis that hold the wall's base, span its width and hold the end of the record box (the body's end moved out by the trim), where they stand beyond the body's end by no more than their own thickness, where no such wall touches the end already, and where they all end at one face (within 0.001 ft). The wall's body is one prism, so it reaches that face at every height: Revit's steps above 182.087 ft and 183.071 ft are not drawn. It runs on the built bodies, before the tops and bases are cut, and the wall carries `BodyEndSource` `wall_end_extended_to_base_wall` and `EndCoverElementId` (the least ElementId of the walls that reach the face). Where those walls end at different faces the wall keeps its end and carries `BodyEndRefusal` `walls_beneath_reach_the_end_at_different_faces`; no wall on any measured model needed it. A wall with no join trim, a body that is not a record box, a wall beneath that is not one rectangle (a stepped profile), or a wall that is not profile-edited is left as it was.
