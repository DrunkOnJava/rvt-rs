# RE-380: Revit writes a profile-edited wall's openings as the pockets of its profile against its convex hull

**Date:** 2026-11-09
**Issues:** #1607 (found by #1600's Measure); follows RE-151 (the openings of a sketch's holes, and the one wall opening of Core Interior), RE-358 (the profile's line carried on past its end) and RE-378 (the profile's highest point as the wall's top)
**Artefacts:** the BIM4LCA office as built and at permit and its terrain files (Revit 2024, CC BY-SA 4.0, each with Revit's own IFC4 export), Autodesk's `2024_Core_Interior.rvt` with Revit's export of it (`research/bim4lca-measure.tsv`, `research/project-corpus.tsv`), and Autodesk's Snowdon Towers Sample Architectural (Revit 2024, sha256 `33271010919acb2a`) with Revit's own IFC4 export of it (sha256 `ecfcb04e3e818090`; local only and not reproducible from the repository, no licence, never committed, as in RE-100; counts and equalities only here)
**Probe:** `probes/examples/probe_1607_wall_profile_lines.rs` (commit `80808447`, archived under `refs/archive/2026-11-09/cited/80808447c8c5`)
**Scorers:** `tools/re/unfilled_openings_vs_ifc.py`, `tools/re/wall_heights_vs_ifc.py`, `tools/re/wall_quantities_vs_ifc.py`, `tools/re/wall_bodies_vs_ifc.py`
**Status:** positive, on 16 pockets of 10 of the 27 walls that have sketch lines in the six files. Revit's export holds the 15 of them it exports (Core Interior's wall 34362 is in neither export) with the pocket's polygon, writes no opening on the 17 other walls, and tags the opening with the first line of the pocket's stretch on 12 of the 15; on the other 3 it names the last line (section 2).

## 1. The question

RE-151's rule (`elevation_profile_cuts`) reads a wall's edited elevation profile, the lines of its sketch in one vertical plane, as its record box less one region for each run of lines that leaves the loop's bounding rectangle, tagged with the run's highest ElementId. It was measured on one wall, Core Interior's 55840, whose one opening in Revit's export it equals in Tag and box. Measure run 42614629885 (all models, `8a5b2225` against `b969296e`) and its scorer run 42618842627 show it writing an opening Revit's export does not write on ten more walls, and missing the one Revit writes on a terrain wall:

## 2. What Revit writes

The probe prints every wall's sketch lines with their ElementIds and ends (run 42619901187 on the five Revit 2024 files with Revit's export, and run 42635378158 on Autodesk's sample projects, which hold Snowdon Towers Sample Architectural). Taking each wall's lines as one loop and computing the loop's convex hull and its pockets, the stretches of the loop between two corners of the hull that the hull's edge cuts off:

| Wall | Pockets | Revit's export |
|---|---|---|
| Core Interior 55840 | 1, eight corners, and the profile rectangle's corner (29.917, 76.0) in the profile's own along-and-up feet | opening Tag 55859 (the stretch's first line), the same box, nine points, 301.649 ft2 |
| Core Interior 34362 | 1, the same as 55840's | in neither export |
| terrain 1387731 | 1, four corners (122.404, 196.522), (138.808, 195.699), (171.639, 192.408), (215.944, 192.408), closed by the hull's edge | opening Tag 1389018 (the stretch's first line), the same four corners and no fifth |
| Snowdon Towers, 7 walls | 13: 7 closed through the rectangle's corner, 6 by the hull's edge | 13 openings, each with its pocket's polygon and none else on these walls; Tag the stretch's first line on 10, its last on the 3 of wall 698364 |
| Snowdon Towers, 2 walls | none | none |
| terrain 1351524 | none (the slope steepens to the right, so the profile is convex) | none |
| office, RE-358's eight | none (each top is one straight sloped line) | none |
| office, 3025796, 3026297, 3027380 | none | none |

**What closes a pocket.** The openings are not closed alike. Core Interior's opening 55859 is nine points in its own plane: the stretch's eight corners and the corner of the profile's bounding rectangle at the wall's far end and top, (29.917, 76.0) in the profile's own along-and-up feet. It holds 301.649 ft2, where the stretch closed by its hull edge holds 71.233. The terrain wall's opening has the stretch's four (x, z) corners and no fifth, where the rectangle's corner (215.944, 196.522) would be one: the hull's edge closes it. The hull edge's ends decide which, and the rule fits all 15 pockets Revit exports. On 55840 they stand on two adjacent sides of the profile's rectangle, the top at (3.583, 76.0) and the far end at (29.917, 58.5), neither at a corner, and the hull's edge cuts the rectangle's top and far-end corner off; the 8 pockets like it (55840's and 7 of Snowdon's) are closed through that corner. On 1387731 one end stands inside the rectangle, (215.944, 192.408), and the other is the rectangle's corner (122.404, 196.522); the 7 pockets that have an end off the rectangle's edge (1387731's and 6 of Snowdon's) are closed by the edge. A notch, where both ends of the edge lie on a side in common, runs the edge along the side and gives the same polygon under any reading (no wall of the six files has one; one wall each of Autodesk's sample projects rac_basic, rme_advanced and rme_basic does, with no Revit export of them in the corpus, and RE-151 wrote an opening for the same polygon), and an edge from a corner to a side it does not share is read neither way (none of the 26 pockets of every Revit 2024 file of the corpus has one). Why Revit closes them so is not shown; the rule is a measurement. Both `unfilled_openings_vs_ifc`'s corner lists (sets of distinct coordinates) and its boxes are the same for the stretch closed either way, which is how the first reading of this report, every pocket closed by its hull edge, passed them: the CI of the first commit of the pull request failed on the test of #227 (`tests/wall_profile_openings.rs`), which reads the opening's area, with rvt-rs's 71.233 ft2 against Revit's 301.649. The scorer now compares the opening's volume (section 4).

**What the Tag names.** An opening's Tag in Revit's export is the ElementId of one line of the pocket's stretch. Walking the stretch counter-clockwise in the profile's own along-and-up coordinates from the hull corner it starts at, Revit names the first line on 12 of the 15 pockets (Core Interior's 55859, the terrain wall's 1389018 and 10 of Snowdon's 13), and the last line on the 3 of Snowdon's wall 698364. The highest id of the stretch, which the first reading of the pull request wrote from the three walls of the five files, is right on 6 of the 15, and the lowest on 8. The sketch list's order does not decide it: Snowdon's wall 1996082 lists two of its stretches against the walk, and wall 1996083 one, and Revit names the first line of each, where on wall 698364 the three stretches are listed against the walk and Revit names the last. What sets 698364 apart is not shown (section 5).

Core Interior's wall 34362 has the same pocket as 55840 and is in neither export (run 42621689708 finds no `IfcWall` with its Tag in Revit's or rvt-rs's). None of Revit's 30 (as built) and 24 (at permit) openings of no cutter in an office wall is on a wall with sketch lines; they are RE-321's rectangular openings. On Snowdon Towers all 9 walls with sketch lines are in Revit's export: the 2 with no pocket have no opening, 4 have one pocket and one opening, and 3 have three and three, 13 openings in all and none the pockets do not predict. So the pocket rule predicts an opening on 10 of the 27 walls with sketch lines (16 pockets) and none on the other 17, and Revit's export holds exactly the 15 pockets it can and none on the 17. RE-151's regions equal the pockets of 55840 and of 4 of Snowdon's 7 walls with pockets (one region each), and are another shape on the terrain wall and the other 3 (two regions each, against three pockets).

Revit writes the body of all 11 exported profile walls of the five files (55840, the office's eight, the terrain's two) as a `Tessellation` (`IfcPolygonalFaceSet`): the final shape, with the pocket's opening beside it where it has one, outside the body and voiding nothing. In Autodesk's open-source exporter (`WallExporter.cs`, `TryToCreateAsExtrusion` and `ExportWall`) a wall is built as an extrusion of its footprint with clippings, and the native call `AddClippingsToBaseExtrusion` hands back the "cut pair" openings that `OpeningUtil.CreateOpeningsIfNecessary` writes; the rule inside it is in `RevitAPIIFC`, closed, so the hull reading is a measurement and not something read. Read that way, the hull's sloped edges are the clip planes, which run on to the wall's end (what #1473 sees as the slope Revit keeps past the profile's end), and a pocket is what no plane can cut.

## 3. What rvt-rs writes

`elevation_profile_pockets` finds the pockets, closes each as section 2 says and tags it with the first line of its stretch in the counter-clockwise walk (pure, with failure-first tests on the numbers of the three walls of the five files, a notch in a side, a hull edge from a corner to a side, which it does not read, and the same lines listed backwards). The regions RE-151 reads are not dropped: they make the wall's shape, the box less the regions, and the passes that move a wall's ends or carry its profile on (RE-348, RE-358) work on them. After every pass that reads a wall's body or openings (`cut_profiles_from_bodies`), each region is dealt with by what Revit writes:

- **A region a pocket equals** (the same corners in the same order from some corner on, within 0.001 ft; Core Interior's 55840 and 4 of Snowdon's 7 walls with pockets) is that pocket. The wall keeps its box, the region's opening is not written and the pocket's is written in its place, so the wall is as RE-151 left it and its opening is Revit's.
- **A region no pocket equals** (the office's eight walls and the terrain's two, none of whose regions is a pocket; the 6 regions of Snowdon's walls 698364, 1996082 and 1996083, two on each against three pockets; and the 2 regions of Snowdon's walls with no pocket) is taken out of the wall's body as an `IfcBooleanResult` difference, the region's outline extruded through the wall and 0.01 ft wider than the wall each side, and written as no opening. The wall's pockets are written as its openings, with the Tag above and the name and GlobalId RE-151 gave the region. The wall carries `ProfileCutSource` `wall_profile_cut_in_body`, counted in `exported.wall_profile_cuts`.

A wall the cut cannot be made for (no body of its own, a representation map, a region that is not a solid) keeps its regions as its openings and writes no pocket, as before. Core Interior's wall 55840 is the first case.

## 5. Not shown

- **The closures not seen.** Three kinds of hull edge are read: one with an end off the rectangle's edge, closed by the edge (7 of the 15); one whose ends lie on a side in common, a notch cut in it, where the edge runs along the side and the polygon is the same under any reading (3 walls of Autodesk's sample projects have one and no Revit export of them is in the corpus; none of the six files with Revit's export has one); and one whose ends lie on two adjacent sides, neither at a corner, closed through the rectangle's corner between them (8 of the 15). A pocket whose hull edge ends otherwise, at a corner of the rectangle with the other end on a side it does not share, is not written, and the wall's body is cut all the same. None of the 26 pockets of every Revit 2024 file of the corpus has one.
- **Why the hull.** The rule is read from 27 walls, 9 of which have an opening in Revit's export. A pocket on a wall outside Revit 2024, with an arc in its profile (RE-151 leaves a sketch with an arc alone), or in a profile with several loops is not measured.
- **The Tag.** The first line of the stretch is Revit's on 12 of the 15 pockets, and the last on the 3 of Snowdon's wall 698364, where rvt-rs's Tag is another line than Revit's: those 3 openings would not pair in `unfilled_openings_vs_ifc`, which pairs an opening by its Tag, and no gate has the file. Revit's polygon for each of these openings starts at the end of the stretch beside the line it names, and the named line is its closing edge on the 12 and its first edge on the 3, so the Tag is the stretch's line at the polygon's first vertex; what fixes that vertex is not read. What was checked, and is the same on all 13 Snowdon openings: the opening's extrusion direction against the wall's own y axis (opposite on all), the polygon's winding in the wall's own along-and-up frame (counter-clockwise on all), and the profile axis against the wall's x axis (the same on all). The sketch list's order does not fit (section 2), a rule by the stretch's highest or lowest id fits less (6 and 8 of 15), and where the polygon starts in its own box (bottom-left, top-right and others) shows no rule. The Tag therefore depends on which end of a sketch the walk starts from (the same lines listed backwards give the line at the other end), and 698364 is the one wall measured where Revit's end is the other.
- **A cut body and an overlapping opening.** IfcOpenShell 0.9.0.post1 (CI's pin) evaluates a wall whose body is a boolean difference and whose pocket's opening lies on the cut as the uncut box on Snowdon Towers' wall 698311 (the volume is the box's, not the box's less the cut, though the body is the difference), and evaluates the same body right with no opening, or with openings that do not overlap the cut. This is why a region a pocket equals is not cut as well as written as an opening. The three Snowdon walls whose two regions each are cut and that hold three pockets (698364, 1996082, 1996083) have a cut body and pockets beside it, and measured equal to the volume of main's body. A cut that overlaps an opening and evaluates wrong may exist outside the models measured, and the scorer's volume line would show it.
- **Wall 34362.** Core Interior's second wall with a pocket is in neither export.
