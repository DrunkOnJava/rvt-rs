## 2. The rule

`profile_extent` (`src/research/partition_schema_mvp/walls.rs`) records, with each wall's profile openings, the lowest and highest points of its sketch along the profile's own axis (`m_profile_extent`). It is read on its own release list, `WALL_PROFILE_STUB_REVIT_VERSIONS` (Revit 2024), as `WALL_END_JOIN_REVIT_VERSIONS` is for the end joins, so that a release added to the list the openings are read on does not carry the rule there. `wall_profile::extend_profile_stubs` (`src/ifc/export_content/wall_profile.rs`) runs after the wall ends and the base cuts, when each wall has its final extent. For each opening of a wall that is one plain extrusion carrying Revit's record property set, at each end of the profile: if the body goes on past the end by more than 0.001 ft and the opening reaches that end with exactly two vertices on one vertical edge, both ends of the edge move out to the wall's end. Two shapes are read, by the neighbours of the edge's two vertices:

- **sloped**: one neighbour is level (an edge of the profile's rectangle) and the other slopes (the profile's last line). The corner moves straight out and the line's end along the line's slope; the cut stays on its side of the line.
- **level**: both neighbours are level, as at the cut-out of a notch. The one on the wall's own top or base is the wall's edge; the other, interior one is the line, and it is carried straight out. The two vertices are told apart by that, not by the order the outline lists them in. Both on the wall's top or base, or both interior, is not read.

A wall of which this is not read keeps the top the opening gives it past the profile, with the reason as `ProfileStubRefusal` (`exported.wall_profile_refusals`, the decision `wall_profile_extension_refused`), the first reason of the wall only (a wall carried at one end and refused at the other is counted in `exported.wall_profile_sources` as well):

- `profile_cut_not_read_at_the_wall_end`: the opening reaches the end with other than one such edge (counted only where the opening comes down into the wall's height there);
- `profile_level_cut_between_two_lines`, `profile_level_cut_spans_the_wall`: the two readings of the level shape above that are not read (the same condition on the wall's height);
- `profile_stub_longer_than_the_wall_is_thick`: a heuristic and not a byte: every stretch measured is half the wall's thickness, and nothing in the file says a wall goes on past its profile by at most its thickness (a join is not what it comes of at the office ends, section 1);
- `profile_slope_leaves_the_cut`: the slope would carry the line to the wall's base or across the rectangle's edge before the wall's end.

A wall whose body goes on past the profile where no opening reaches that end, or where the cut-out stays outside the wall across the stretch, is not changed and not counted. A wall that carries the change has `ProfileStubSource` `wall_profile_<what>_past_<where>`: `<what>` is `sloped`, `level` or `sloped_and_level`, `<where>` is `the_low_end`, `the_high_end` or `both_ends` of the profile along its own axis (`exported.wall_profile_sources`).
