## 1. What the bytes say

RE-336 cuts a wall's base at the top of the footing its base join names (`JoinToRoofGStep` with `m_baseOrTop` 0) when that element covers the whole of the wall's plan. The office's Basement walls 2058499 and 2058501 as built name the footing 2058504, which covers their plan only in part: it begins at y 75.9678 ft, and the walls' plan (y 75.7285 to 80.3806 ft, 75.4183 ft once RE-337 carries their ends on to Revit's) begins before it. Revit's export gives the body that results. In the frame of rvt-rs's export (Revit's export turned 40.33 degrees about the origin, which fits the vertices of the two footings to 1e-3 ft), the vertices of Revit's wall 2058499 over the end are:

- from the footing's low edge (y 75.9678 ft) towards the far end, the base follows the footing's sloped top: 174.8688 ft at the edge to 176.8373 ft at y 80.3806 ft;
- over the 0.55 ft of plan before the edge, the base is the wall's own, 174.2126 ft, and a vertical face of 0.66 ft joins the two at the edge (174.2126 to 174.8688 ft).

That is the wall minus the footing: where the two share plan the wall starts at the footing's top, and where they do not it starts at its own base. Revit's lowest point for both walls is therefore the wall's own base (174.2126 ft), which `main` already had because it kept the base whole, and its volume is 30.805 ft3, which `main`'s whole prism (35.302 ft3) is 4.497 ft3 over.

## 2. The rule

`decide_base` (`wall_tops.rs`) takes an element named by the base join that covers the wall in part as well as one that covers it whole: its outline over the wall's plan (`reach` over `STEP_TOLERANCE_SQUARE_FEET`) gives the convex pieces of the plan inside it (`covered_pieces`: the wall's convex pieces clipped by the triangles of the outline, joined where two make a convex piece). The element's top over the covered pieces only must lie between the wall's base and top, as for a whole cover (a top that crosses either is refused as before), and only one element may qualify. An element that covers the whole plan decides first: a wall whose base join names any element that covers the whole plan takes no cut over part of it, whether or not that cover came to a cut (its top crossed the wall's base or top, say), so no wall that `main` cut or refused over a whole cover changes, and a wall naming one that covers it whole and one that covers part, both with a top between its base and top, is cut at the first, as on `main`. Neither measured wall names an element that covers it whole: both are still cut in part in the run at `e5ff94f4`, which holds this. `base_cut_solid` then makes, for each piece of the wall (its whole plan, or the pieces of a wall whose ends RE-337 carried on), the part inside the covered pieces cut at the element's top (along the plane of a shape-edited top, or at its level) and the rest of the piece as a column from the wall's own base. A wall carries `BodyBaseSource` `wall_base_sloped_at_floor_covering_in_part` or `wall_base_cut_at_floor_covering_in_part` (a level top), and `BaseCoverElementId`. Where the covered part is not read (the wall's plan is not made of convex pieces, a hole or opening of the element lies over the wall, two of the covered pieces overlap because the element's loops or its outline's triangles do, nothing is inside, or the part falls in more than 32 pieces) the wall keeps its base and says `partly_covered_plan_not_read` as its `BaseCoverRefusal`.

RE-337's end rule asks whether the cut of the base still resolves over the longer plan. It now does for these two walls, so they take Revit's end with the base cut.

## 4. Not measured

Nothing but the office has been measured on this head. These are exercised by no measured wall, so no Revit export has checked them:

- the level top of a partial cover (`wall_base_cut_at_floor_covering_in_part`; the two walls are cut along a sloped top);
- an element with a hole or opening under the wall (refused, not scored), and a wall whose plan is not convex (refused);
- a wall covered in part by an element in a model other than the office as built: Core Interior, RE1 Architecture, the 24 Revit 2025 models, Autodesk's sample projects and the held-out files are to be compared with `main` before this lands, and a wall they move that Revit's export does not confirm is a reason to narrow the rule;
- a footing that covers a wall's two ends and not its middle (the covered pieces would be two);
- a partially covered wall without an end extension (both measured walls take RE-337's end, which is what carried their plan past the footing's edge by 0.55 ft; the record's plan leaves 0.20 ft2 outside it, and the cut of that plan alone is not measured);
- the Level branch (a flat floor or footing covering a wall in part): no measured wall has one;
- a wall or an element whose ring runs clockwise or is concave: the second read made both safe in the code (the ring is turned counter-clockwise and cut into convex pieces, and the parts must add up to the piece), and neither has a measured instance, since both measured walls are rectangles with a counter-clockwise ring;
- walls or elements with an arc or a concave ring: the ring is cut into convex pieces and each piece becomes one body item, so a 90 degree arc of a wall has on the order of a hundred items (by reading, not measured), and the cap of 32 pieces applies only to the covered pieces, not to the wall's; no measured wall is curved or concave;
- a wall whose cut resolves and whose solid cannot be made (`base_cut_solid_not_made`): no measured wall;
- the sliver floor, `reach` over 1e-4 ft2 (RE-320's step tolerance): the least part measured is the 0.20 ft2 that the footing leaves of the record's plan, so nothing says where the floor should be.
